# 19 - Website feedback: abstractdocs.com against the ratified strategy

The site went up fast to get the waitlist live, and it is genuinely good: the positioning is document-first, the tone is anti-hype, the "felt the ceiling" framing is exactly the ratified target market, and the honest beta labels are the right instinct. This document reviews it against everything ratified in docs 13-18 and recommends changes. The owner is open to rebuilding entirely; the recommendation below is **revise, don't rebuild** - the bones are right, and the changes are copy and one new page, not architecture.

## 1. What the site already gets right (keep)

- **The market framing.** "You use Claude or ChatGPT every day. You've felt the ceiling." - this *is* the ratified beta market (doc 14 §2.1), articulated before the planning session formalised it. Keep it prominent.
- **The manifesto.** "Documents are the work. It's time the tools believed that." - keep verbatim; it is the category argument in one line.
- **The three pillars** (project-scope agency / every change is a proposal / living documents) map cleanly to killer flows ② ① ③ and the wedge. Keep the structure.
- **The trust-first GTM voice.** "Bring a real project. The first document is the demo." - now literally the beta motion (doc 18 §4). Keep.
- **Liberal beta labels and honest language** ("no change recommended, not invented news"). Keep and extend (see §3.4).
- **The founder note.** Authentic, specific (ISO 27001, 24 policies), and the solo-with-AI-tools story practices what the product preaches. Keep.

## 2. The strategic gap: the ITE story is missing

The site sells the **wedge** (trust in recurring multi-doc work) and never mentions the **category** (the Integrated Thinking Environment - doc 14 §1). The visitor who would love Abstract most - the strategy thinker who wants to *think* in it daily, not just refresh reports - has no page speaking to them.

**Recommendation: add a fourth pillar or a dedicated section - "A place to think".** Copy direction (to be written in the site's own voice):

- The capability line: *what Claude Code gave technical people in the terminal - skills, agents, your whole corpus as context - inside a word processor, with the technical stuff abstracted away.*
- The experience line: *be interviewed about your idea; have your thinking stress-tested; run a brainstorm as a process, not a prayer - and every suggested change still arrives as a diff you approve.*
- The loop line: *think and build here with all your context; export or publish the result into whatever your organisation uses.*

Placement: after the three trust pillars, not before them - category behind wedge, matching the strategy (doc 14 §1: lead with trust, let thinking be the depth that keeps them). A use-case page "Strategy & thinking" alongside the existing "Strategy & research" would carry the long version.

## 3. Specific corrections (conflicts with ratified decisions)

### 3.1 Pricing FAQ

Current: "Paid plans will be per-seat with usage on top." Ratified (doc 14 §5): the Cursor model - **per-seat with a bundled usage allowance included**; only heavy usage pays on top. The difference matters to exactly this audience (they know what Cursor charges and why). Suggested FAQ copy: *"Free while in beta. Paid plans will be per-seat with generous usage included - heavy automation pays for what it uses on top. Early users get preferential pricing."*

### 3.2 Publish: static, not live

Current: publish framed under living documents as a page that stays current ("re-renders on every approved change"). Ratified (D17, conflict C1 in doc 13): **published artifacts are static snapshots** stamped "verified as of {date}"; when the doc changes, the owner is prompted to Republish - sync is a deliberate human act. Fix the copy to sell the *stronger* claim: a published page that can **prove what it said and when** - the auditor-grade story - rather than a live page nobody governs.

### 3.3 Model access honesty (beta FAQ)

The beta will ask users to sign in with their ChatGPT subscription, with a founder-funded fallback (doc 18 §2.1). Add a plain FAQ entry: *"What does it run on? Bring your ChatGPT subscription and Abstract uses it directly; if you don't have one, we cover a capable default model during beta."* Do not promise Claude subscription support - it is currently prohibited by Anthropic's terms for third-party tools.

### 3.4 Expectation management for a golden-path alpha

The site's demo confidence exceeds the app's off-path robustness (doc 18's honest starting point). Two cheap mitigations:

- A short "Where we honestly are" note in the FAQ or founder note: early beta, working closely with each user, some paths still rough - which converts fragility into intimacy (and matches the gradual-rollout posture already stated).
- Audit each feature claim against the re-baselined journey chips (doc 13 §6) once the journey walk is done; anything the walk grades below walkable gets a "coming" label, matching the honesty the site already shows for triggers and webhooks.

### 3.5 Vocabulary alignment

Ensure the site uses the ratified user-facing nouns - Skills, Agents, Sources, Templates (doc 17 §2) - consistently. The agent library and slash-command features already shown fit this; align any stray synonyms ("routines", "automations") to the four nouns.

## 4. Additions worth making (not corrections)

- **The aha, above the fold.** The provenance peek demos in 5 seconds (killer flow ③); an inline animated peek near the hero would put the trust moment before the first scroll. The current screen demos are good but live further down.
- **A "your files stay yours" strip.** P6 is the adoption lever ("the escape hatch is the sales pitch" - map Ø); the site mentions plain files but doesn't sell walk-away-anytime as a first-class promise.
- **Onboarding survey questions doubled into the waitlist form** (daily-driver model, subscriptions owned, what you make weekly) - the waitlist becomes cohort research before anyone is onboarded (doc 18 §2.4).
- **Local-first as a feature.** "Runs on your machine; your documents never leave it unless you publish" is both true today and a differentiator against every cloud AI tool - and it sets up the chapter-2 cloud story as an upgrade, not a correction.

## 5. Priority order

1. Pricing FAQ fix (§3.1) and publish copy fix (§3.2) - small, and they prevent making promises the product has decided against.
2. Model-access FAQ (§3.3) and expectation note (§3.4) - required before the first cohort reads the site as a contract.
3. The ITE section (§2) - the strategic gap; do it when the thinking-skills pack exists so the section can show, not tell.
4. Additions (§4) in any order, waitlist-survey first (it compounds: every early signup answers it).
