Product principles — P0–P10 (P0–P2 Tom’s, 9 Jul; P3–P10 drawn from the decisions — ratified)

P0 Trust above all. The generation must be correct — and feel MORE reliable than Claude Desktop or ChatGPT. That is the bar, and it is why every other principle exists: grounding in sources (P4), receipts (P8), review gates (P3), and honest confidence labels. When correctness and speed conflict, correctness wins. When correctness and delight conflict, correctness IS the delight.
P1 Progressive disclosure. Surface a capability only when the user’s situation calls for it. Depth is earned, never dumped.
P2 No duplicate pathways. If a job already has a clean route, don’t build a second one for v1 (e.g. D20: templates already cover recurring creation).
P3 Everything routes through review. Chat, fan-out, agents, hooks — one proposal grammar, no second-class writes.
P4 No hidden context. What the agent sees is always inspectable before it acts (task context in the tab, doc sources in the rail).
P5 Plain words. “While I’m away…”, not cron; “heavy context”, not token counts. Git-shaped mechanics stay internal (D7’s commit is a metaphor, never UI copy).
P6 Files are the truth. Plain, portable, rebuildable — the user can always walk away with their folder.
P7 Calm by default. Mechanical work happens silently with receipts; only judgement calls interrupt a human.
P8 Receipts over promises. Every change carries who/what/why/when; undo works everywhere, including across approves.
P9 The human dials autonomy. Trust is granted per doc and per agent (D9), never assumed — and can be taken back.
P10 Boring excellence first. The editor must feel Word-grade (paste, tables, images) before any magic matters — weakness here disqualifies everything else.

The customer — “the accountable operator”

One person, three faces. They don’t want a document tool; they want to stop being afraid of their own documents.

Compliance manager (the ISMS sample) — 24 policies, quarterly audits, decisions land in meetings and must ripple through everything. Fear: an auditor finds a stale clause.
Fund ops / IR lead — LP letters and board notes where every number is checkable by someone with money at stake. Fear: shipping last quarter’s figure.
Chief of staff / ops generalist — the weekly pack: assembles other people’s numbers into recurring narrative. Fear: Sunday night.
↳ Shared traits: owns RECURRING docs whose numbers live elsewhere · pays in reputation, not time · lives in Word · not technical — every git/token concept costs us a user (P5).
↳ Success in their words: “I never ship a stale number” · “Monday takes 90 seconds, not Sunday night” · “I can prove any figure on demand.”

The killer flows — rank order

Three moments carry the product. Everything else must not embarrass them.

① The morning all-clear (1w+1r) — the HABIT. Overnight work → 90-second review → “all clear”. This is why they come back tomorrow.
② Transcript fan-out → cross-doc review (1j→1k) — the DEMO. One meeting recording updates 24 policies with receipts. This is why they tell their boss.
③ The provenance peek (1p) — the TRUST moment. Hover a number, see its source and freshness. This is what they show a colleague first — it demos in 5 seconds.
↳ Delight = relief, not fireworks: the product’s emotional payload is “nothing is silently wrong”. Design ① to feel like inbox-zero, ③ to feel like x-ray vision.
✔ Decided (Tom, 9 Jul): onboarding = demo CSV → chat generates a report from it (③ provenance from minute one) → then prompts ONE iteration on the doc so the user also sees a single inline diff (1e). Two wow moments, ten minutes, no setup. → D26, test T5

Adoption & love — the levers

Usable → valuable → loveable is a sequence, not a menu. Each stage has one lever.

Usable: first 10 minutes — open folder → agent orients (2b) → first approved change in <3 min. No account-config wall, no empty states that lecture.
Valuable: the first Monday — a schedule set in week 1 (1q) pays out as ① within 7 days. Design the week-one arc, not just screens.
Adoptable: zero lock-in anxiety — files stay theirs (P6), export to Word keeps colleagues happy (1u). The escape hatch IS the sales pitch.
Loveable: receipts + calm (P7/P8) — the product never nags, never surprises, always shows its work.
✔ Agreed (Tom, 9 Jul): aha metric = first approved AGENT change on the user’s OWN file (not sample data). Measure to confirm it predicts retention — logged as T4.
